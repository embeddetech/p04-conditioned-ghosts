\begin{abstract}
Local lossy (e.g.\ non-Hermitian) evolution on one arm of an entangled
pair cannot signal; conditioning on survival famously produces an
apparent signal, whose location in the post-selection is by now well
understood. What has been missing is its \emph{price}. We prove a
sharp trade-off: for a qubit pair prepared in a Bell state, Alice's
message encoded as a survival effect $M$ versus its spatial flip
$XMX$, the total-variation distance between Bob's survival-conditioned
marginals obeys
\[
\TV^{2}\;+\;\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\det M}}{p}\Bigr)^{2}\;\le\;1 ,
\]
where $p$ is the survival probability. For dynamics generated by a
lossy Hamiltonian, $\sqrt{\det M}=|\det C|=\exp[-\tfrac12\!\int_0^t\!\Tr\Gamma(s)\,
ds]$ is the accumulated total-loss factor---a message-independent
invariant---so the conditioned signal is bounded exactly by the
\emph{anisotropy} of the loss, and anisotropy forces the total-loss
factor strictly below the survival probability. The
balanced gain/loss ($\PT$) dimer saturates the bound at every coupling
and every time, in both phases: broken-$\PT$ dynamics traces the
extremal frontier of post-selected signaling. The classical
(configuration-level) signal obeys the \emph{same} bound with a
strictly smaller equality set (uncoupled loss), and the two signals
close in exact quadrature,
$\TV^{2}=\TV_{\rm cl}^{2}+\chi^{2}$ with $\chi$ the conditioned
flip-odd coherence; a coupled broken-phase dimer therefore pays a
strict classical deficit, and its classical asymptote reproduces the
closed form $\tilde\kappa/\sin\theta$ obtained earlier as a corollary
of the terminal-indivisibility theorem. We further prove a
fidelity-type bound for arbitrary pure inputs and unequal survival
probabilities that reduces exactly to the sharp bound at the Bell
state, and a general-dimension bound via Lidskii's theorem, and we
report a numerically established failure: the Bell state is
\emph{not} extremal at fixed invariants. All statements are verified
numerically, including $\sim1.8\times10^{5}$ randomized violation
searches.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Lee, Hsieh, Flammia, and Lee showed that local $\PT$-symmetric
evolution on one arm of an entangled pair, treated as fundamental,
permits superluminal signaling \cite{LHFL2014}. The resolution
literature has located the apparent signal precisely: it was pinned
experimentally to the post-selected (survival-conditioned)
subensemble \cite{Tang2016}, consistency is restored in
metric-corrected and embedded treatments \cite{Brody2016,Ju2019},
and in a companion paper we reproduced the full bookkeeping in
classical-probability terms and computed the signal's closed-form
magnitude for the broken-$\PT$ dimer \emph{at the configuration
(beable) level}, $\TV_{\rm cl}(t\to\infty)=\tilde\kappa/\sin\theta$
\cite{instantaneous}. A parallel literature quantifies what
post-selection can buy in metrology: success probability trades
against Fisher information \cite{CFJC2014,AS2020}, and asymptotic
post-selected hypothesis testing is governed by projective metrics
\cite{RKW2011,RLW2022}. But the elementary single-shot question
underlying the $\PT$ controversy appears never to have been posed as
such: \emph{how large can the conditioned signal be, given the
survival probability?} (As we show in
Remark~\ref{rem:hilbert}, the core inequality can in fact be
assembled from the Hilbert-projective-metric toolkit of Reeb,
Kastoryano, and Wolf \cite{RKW2011}---whose post-selection results
are otherwise deliberately blind to the success probability---but
the statement, its invariant, and its saturation structure appear
nowhere.)

This paper answers it with a sharp inequality
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}) whose right-hand side involves one further
invariant: the determinant of Alice's survival effect. Three features
seem worth advertising in advance. First, the bound is
\emph{Pythagorean}: signal$^2$ plus (normalized total-loss
factor)$^2$ cannot exceed one. Second, it is exactly saturated, at
every instant of time, by the balanced gain/loss dimer that is the
standard laboratory system of experimental $\PT$ optics---the
dynamics at the center of the controversy is the extremal case of
the phenomenon. Third, the same right-hand side bounds the
\emph{classical} (configuration-level) signal, and the two signals
obey an exact Pythagorean decomposition whose third side is the
conditioned coherence: a quantitative statement of how much of the
post-selected anomaly is intrinsically quantum.

Everything below is stated for two-level systems first (the
experimentally relevant case), then generalized. Numerical
verification of every claim, including $\sim1.8\times10^{5}$
randomized violation searches, is in the public script
(see Code availability).

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Alice and Bob share the Bell state
$\ket{\Phi}=(\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt2$. Alice applies a
trace-nonincreasing operation and announces nothing; the operation's
\emph{survival effect} is $M$ with $0\le M\le\mathbf 1$ (for a
single-Kraus filter $C$, $M=C^{\dagger}C$; for lossy Hamiltonian
evolution, $C=\ee^{-\ii H_{\rm eff}t}$ with
$H_{\rm eff}=H-\tfrac{\ii}{2}\Gamma$, $\Gamma\ge0$). Her binary
message is the \emph{orientation} of the loss: effect $M_{1}=M$
versus its spatial flip $M_{2}=XMX$, $X=\sigma_x$ (the geometry of
\cite{LHFL2014} and of the lossy-coupler experiments
\cite{Tang2016}). Both messages have the same survival probability
\begin{equation}
p=\tfrac12\Tr M
\qquad(\Tr XMX=\Tr M),
\end{equation}
and the same effect determinant $\det M$. Bob's
survival-conditioned marginals are
$\sigma_{i}=M_{i}^{T}/(2p)$, and the signal is their trace
distance, $\TV=\tfrac12\norm{\sigma_1-\sigma_2}_1$---the maximal
distinguishability over all of Bob's measurements. Unconditioned,
Bob's marginal is exactly message-independent \cite{instantaneous}.

\section{The trade-off theorem}
\label{sec:main}

\begin{theorem}[survival--distinguishability trade-off]
\label{thm:main}
With the setup above,
\begin{equation}
\TV\;\le\;\sqrt{1-\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\det M}}{p}\Bigr)^{2}} ,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\TV^{2}+\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{\det M}}{p}\Bigr)^{2}\le 1 ,
\label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
with equality iff the eigenvectors of $M$ are exchanged by $X$
(equivalently: the Bloch vector of $M$ is orthogonal to the $x$
axis).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu_1^{2}\ge\mu_2^{2}$ be the eigenvalues of $M$, so
$\mu_1^{2}+\mu_2^{2}=2p$ and $\mu_1^{2}\mu_2^{2}=\det M$. The
operator $\Delta=M-XMX$ is Hermitian and traceless, so its
eigenvalues are $\pm\lambda$ and
$\norm{\Delta}_1=2\lambda$. For any unit vector $v$,
\[
v^{\dagger}\Delta v
=v^{\dagger}Mv-(Xv)^{\dagger}M(Xv)
\le \mu_1^{2}-\mu_2^{2},
\]
since $v^{\dagger}Mv\le\mu_1^{2}$ and
$(Xv)^{\dagger}M(Xv)\ge\mu_2^{2}$; hence
$\lambda\le\mu_1^{2}-\mu_2^{2}$. Therefore
\[
\TV=\frac{\norm{M_1-M_2}_1}{4p}
\le\frac{\mu_1^{2}-\mu_2^{2}}{2p}
=\frac{\sqrt{(\mu_1^{2}+\mu_2^{2})^{2}-4\mu_1^{2}\mu_2^{2}}}{2p}
=\sqrt{1-\frac{\det M}{p^{2}}}.
\]
Equality in the variational step requires $Mv=\mu_1^{2}v$ and
$M(Xv)=\mu_2^{2}(Xv)$: $X$ maps the top eigenvector to the bottom
one. In Bloch form $M=m_0\mathbf 1+\vec n\cdot\vec\sigma$, the
condition is $n_x=0$ ($X$-conjugation then reverses $\vec n$
exactly).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the invariant]
For Hamiltonian-generated dynamics,
\[
\sqrt{\det M}=|\det C|
=\exp\bigl[-\tfrac12\textstyle\int_0^t\Tr\Gamma(s)\,ds\bigr]
\]
is the accumulated
total-loss factor (the determinant-level survival): it does not
depend on the message (flipping the
loss orientation preserves the trace of $\Gamma$), it is monotone
in time, and it satisfies $|\det C|\le p$ always (AM--GM), with
equality iff the loss is \emph{isotropic} ($C$ proportional to a
unitary), in which case the bound forces $\TV=0$. The theorem
therefore says, operationally: \emph{the conditioned signal is
bounded by the anisotropy of the loss}---the normalized spread of
the survival eigenvalues---\emph{and at a fixed survival probability,
anisotropy is paid for in accumulated loss}: $|\det C|/p$ is exactly
$\sqrt{1-(\text{bound})^{2}}$, so the total-loss factor sits strictly
below $p$ unless the loss is isotropic. For
non-invertible filters (rank-deficient effects, e.g.\ projectors), $\det M=0$ and the bound is
vacuous: this correctly reproduces the elementary fact that static
orthogonal filters achieve $\TV=1$ at any $p\le\tfrac12$. The
inequality has content precisely for dynamics.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Hilbert-metric reading, and what is due to
Reeb--Kastoryano--Wolf]
\label{rem:hilbert}
The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:main} is a projective-metric
quantity in disguise: with $d_H$ Hilbert's projective metric on the
positive cone \cite{RKW2011},
$\sqrt{1-\det M/p^{2}}=(\mu_1^{2}-\mu_2^{2})/(\mu_1^{2}+\mu_2^{2})
=\tanh\bigl[d_H(M,\mathbf 1)/2\bigr]$---the Hilbert distance of the
survival effect from the identity. Indeed, the inequality itself
can be assembled from published results of \cite{RKW2011}: their
Prop.~7 (in the arXiv version) gives $\TV\le\tanh[d_H(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)/4]$, $d_H$ is
projective and transpose-invariant (so the steering map costs nothing) and
$X$-conjugation is a $d_H$-isometry fixing $\mathbf 1$, whence
$d_H(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\le2d_H(M,\mathbf 1)$ by the triangle
inequality. Their only post-selection statements, however, are
probability-blind by construction ($d_H$ mods out normalization;
their Thm.~21 quantifies the success probabilities away), the
steering scenario never appears, and neither does the invariant
$\det M/p^{2}$, the saturation analysis, or the decomposition of
Sec.~\ref{sec:classical}. We therefore claim the statement, its
operational reading, and its equality structure---not the
underlying contraction technology, which is theirs (and, for the
$X$-flipped pair, the inequality also follows from an elementary
Bloch-sphere argument: both conditioned states have Bloch radius
$\sqrt{1-\det M/p^{2}}$, and their distance is maximal when the
vectors are antipodal).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Broken-\texorpdfstring{$\PT$}{PT} dynamics traces the extremal frontier}

The equality set of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is the set of effects
with $n_x=0$; what distinguishes the $\PT$ dimer is that it sits in
this set \emph{dynamically}, along its entire trajectory. The
canonical dimer of $\PT$ optics is
$H_{\rm eff}=\cos\theta\,\mathbf 1+\ii\sin\theta\,\sigma_z
+s\,\sigma_x-\ii\sin\theta\,\mathbf 1$ (loss frame), with
$a=\sin\theta$ the loss-splitting rate, $s$ the coupling, and
exceptional point at $s=a$ \cite{indivisible}. Writing
$c=\cosh\tilde\kappa t$,
$h=\sinh(\tilde\kappa t)/\tilde\kappa$,
$\tilde\kappa=\sqrt{a^{2}-s^{2}}$ (broken phase; trigonometric
continuation in the unbroken phase), a two-line computation gives
the survival effect in Bloch form:
\begin{equation}
M=\ee^{-2at}\Bigl[(c^{2}+(a^{2}{+}s^{2})h^{2})\,\mathbf 1
+2ac\,h\,\sigma_z+2as\,h^{2}\,\sigma_y\Bigr].
\label{eq:dimerbloch}
\end{equation}
The Bloch vector lies in the $y$--$z$ plane, $n_x=0$, \emph{at every
time, every coupling, and in both phases}: by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the dimer saturates the bound identically
along its whole trajectory. (Numerically: $|\TV-\text{bound}|\le
1.3\times10^{-15}$ across the broken phase and
$\le4\times10^{-13}$ in the unbroken phase; a search over
$99{,}846$ random lossy generators found no violation of
\eqref{eq:main}, with minimum margin $3\times10^{-10}$---the bound
is approached arbitrarily closely but never crossed.)

In the broken phase $|\det C|/p\sim\ee^{-2\tilde\kappa t}\to0$, so
the bound---and with it the quantum signal---tends to $1$: deep
broken-$\PT$ dynamics extracts the maximum possible conditioned
signal permitted at its survival cost, at every instant. In the
unbroken phase $p$ oscillates at the Rabi period while $|\det C|$
decays smoothly; the signal tracks the resulting oscillating bound
exactly.

\section{The classical (beable) signal: same bound, different
extremal family}
\label{sec:classical}

In the stochastic-process reading of the companion papers
\cite{indivisible,instantaneous}, Bob's physical record is his
configuration, so the natural signal is the total-variation distance
between the conditioned \emph{configuration distributions}---the
diagonals of $\sigma_{1,2}$:
$\TV_{\rm cl}=|M_{11}-M_{22}|/(2p)$ for the $X$-flipped pair.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:classical}
$\TV_{\rm cl}$ obeys the same bound \eqref{eq:main}, with equality
iff $M$ is diagonal ($n_x=n_y=0$): the classical extremal family is
\emph{uncoupled anisotropic loss} ($s=0$), for which
$\TV_{\rm cl}=\tanh(2at)$ and $\sqrt{\det M}/p=\mathrm{sech}(2at)$
saturate the Pythagorean identity exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$M_{11}+M_{22}=2p$ and $M_{11}M_{22}\ge\det M$ (the off-diagonal
term only lowers the determinant), so
$(M_{11}-M_{22})^{2}\le4p^{2}-4\det M$; equality iff $M_{12}=0$.
\end{proof}

The two signals are related exactly. For \emph{every} effect and
flip pair, $M-XMX=2(n_y\sigma_y+n_z\sigma_z)$, so
$\TV=\sqrt{n_y^{2}+n_z^{2}}/p$ and $\TV_{\rm cl}=|n_z|/p$:

\begin{corollary}[Pythagorean decomposition of the signal]
\label{cor:pythagoras}
$\TV^{2}=\TV_{\rm cl}^{2}+\chi^{2}$, where
$\chi=|n_y|/p=2\,|\mathrm{Im}\bra{0}\sigma_1\ket{1}|$ is the
conditioned flip-odd coherence. (Verified to $4.4\times10^{-16}$
over $2000$ random effects.)
\end{corollary}

Consequences. (i) $\TV_{\rm cl}\le\TV$ always; a \emph{coupled}
dimer, whose Bloch vector has $n_y\propto as h^{2}\neq0$ for $t>0$,
saturates the quantum bound but sits strictly inside the classical
one throughout the broken phase (in the unbroken phase the deficit
closes at the isolated Rabi nodes $h=0$, where signal and bound
vanish together)---and by Corollary~\ref{cor:pythagoras} the
deficit is carried, in quadrature, exactly by the conditioned
coherence $\chi$. (ii) Along the broken dimer,
$\TV_{\rm cl}(t\to\infty)=\tilde\kappa/\sin\theta
=\sqrt{1-(s/\sin\theta)^{2}}$, the closed form of
\cite{instantaneous}, now situated: it is the classical shadow of a
quantum signal that saturates its bound at $1$. In the beable
currency the post-selected anomaly is strictly smaller than in the
quantum currency, and the gap is a measurable function of the
coupling.

\section{Generalizations}
\label{sec:general}

\begin{proposition}[arbitrary pure inputs, unequal survival]
\label{prop:fidelity}
Let the shared state be any pure two-qubit state with Bob marginal
$\rho_B$, and let Alice's messages be effects $M_1,M_2$ (not
necessarily flip-related) with survival probabilities $p_i$. Then
\begin{equation}
\TV\;\le\;\sqrt{1-\frac{4\det\rho_B\,
\sqrt{\det M_1\det M_2}}{p_1p_2}} .
\label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing the state as $\psi$ (a $2\times2$ matrix of amplitudes)
with $\psi^{T}=\sqrt{\rho_B}\,U$ by polar decomposition, the common
unitary $U$ can be absorbed into a relabeling
$\tilde M_i:=UM_i^{T}U^{\dagger}$ of Alice's effects, which changes
neither $\det M_i$ ($\det\tilde M_i=\det M_i$) nor the survival
probabilities; without loss of generality Bob's conditioned states
are $\sigma_i=\sqrt{\rho_B}\,\tilde M_i\sqrt{\rho_B}/p_i$ with
$p_i=\Tr(\rho_B\tilde M_i)$, so
$\det\sigma_i=\det\rho_B\det M_i/p_i^{2}$. For qubits
$F(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)^{2}=\Tr\sigma_1\sigma_2
+2\sqrt{\det\sigma_1\det\sigma_2}$ \cite{QubitFidelity}, and by the
rearrangement inequality
$\Tr\sigma_1\sigma_2\ge\lambda_1\nu_2+\lambda_2\nu_1
\ge2\sqrt{\det\sigma_1\det\sigma_2}$ (eigenvalues
$\lambda_i,\nu_i$; AM--GM), so
$F^{2}\ge4\sqrt{\det\sigma_1\det\sigma_2}$; conclude with
$\TV\le\sqrt{1-F^{2}}$ \cite{FvdG1999}.
\end{proof}

For the Bell state ($\det\rho_B=\tfrac14$) and flip-related
messages, \eqref{eq:fidelity} reduces \emph{exactly} to the sharp
bound \eqref{eq:main}: the general proposition interpolates to the
main theorem. (Verified: $0$ violations in $39{,}460$ random draws
mixing flip-related and fully independent message pairs.) We record
a numerically established \emph{negative}: the
natural conjecture that the Bell state is extremal at fixed
$(\sqrt{|\det C_1||\det C_2|},\sqrt{p_1p_2})$ is \textbf{false}---randomized search
finds partially entangled states exceeding the Bell envelope at
those nominal invariants by as much as $0.81$ (while, of course,
respecting \eqref{eq:fidelity}); the $\det\rho_B$ dependence in
\eqref{eq:fidelity} is real, not an artifact of the proof.

\begin{proposition}[general dimension]
\label{prop:generald}
Let the shared state be maximally entangled in dimension $d$, so
that Bob's conditioned states are $\sigma_i=M_i^{T}/\Tr M$, and let
the messages be $M_1=M$ and $M_2=GMG^{\dagger}$ for any unitary
relabeling $G$. With $\mu^{2}_{(1)}\ge\dots\ge
\mu^{2}_{(d)}$ the eigenvalues of $M$:
\begin{equation}
\TV\;\le\;\frac{1}{2\Tr M}\sum_{i=1}^{d}
\bigl|\mu^{2}_{(i)}-\mu^{2}_{(d+1-i)}\bigr| .
\label{eq:generald}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lidskii's theorem the eigenvalues of $M_1-M_2$ are majorized by
the vector $\mu^{2\downarrow}-\mu^{2\uparrow}$ of anti-sorted
differences \cite{Bhatia}; the trace norm is Schur-convex on
traceless vectors, giving
$\norm{M_1-M_2}_1\le\sum_i|\mu^{2}_{(i)}-\mu^{2}_{(d+1-i)}|$; divide
by $2\Tr M$.
\end{proof}

For $d=2$ this recovers \eqref{eq:main} exactly. Verified: $0$
violations in $2\times10^{4}$ random draws each at $d=3,4$, with
both Haar-random and permutation relabelings (minimum margins
$1.7\times10^{-6}$, $1.1\times10^{-6}$).

\section{Experimental note}
\label{sec:experiment}

Both axes of the trade-off are directly measurable on existing
entangled-photon platforms with tailored loss
\cite{Tang2016,Ehrhardt2022}: $p$ is the heralded survival rate,
$\TV$ is read from Bob's conditioned tomography (or, for
$\TV_{\rm cl}$, from his basis statistics alone), and
$\sqrt{\det M}$ is fixed by the device's total loss budget. A
broken-$\PT$ coupler swept in propagation length traces the extremal
frontier $\TV^{2}+(\sqrt{\det M}/p)^{2}=1$ point by point;
any engineered deviation from the balanced dimer form (e.g.\
$n_x\neq0$ loss) must fall measurably inside it. The experimental-proposal paper
(\url{https://github.com/embeddetech/p02-measurable-ghosts})
specifies a Klauck-class device and error budget for
the closely related process-level observables of \cite{indivisible};
the present frontier measurement can share hardware and data with
it.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{What is and is not claimed.} The bound governs
survival-conditioned distinguishability; it does not challenge
no-signaling, since building the conditioned ensemble requires
Alice's survival record, which travels classically
\cite{Tang2016,Ju2019,instantaneous}. What it adds to the resolution
literature is the quantitative layer: the post-selected anomaly has
a hard ceiling set by loss anisotropy, the ceiling is met by the
very dynamics that generated the controversy, and the classical
share of the anomaly is separated from the coherent share by the
same invariant. In the language of the companion program, where
survival conditioning is one of exactly two entry points for
apparent superluminality in a beables ontology \cite{instantaneous},
this paper prices that entry point.

\emph{Relation to prior trade-offs.} Post-selected metrology bounds
success-probability-weighted Fisher information \cite{CFJC2014,AS2020}; those are local (parameter-estimation) statements and do
not imply single-shot distinguishability bounds. Asymptotic
post-selected discrimination is governed by the Hilbert projective
metric \cite{RKW2011,RLW2022}, and Remark~\ref{rem:hilbert} makes
the debt precise: the contraction technology is
Reeb--Kastoryano--Wolf's, while the survival-probability trade-off,
the invariant $\det M/p^{2}$, the steering formulation, and the
saturation structure are new here. The optimal-filter
literature (hidden nonlocality \cite{Gisin1996}) maximizes
post-selected nonlocality without a survival constraint; here the
survival constraint is the point.

\emph{Open questions.} (i) A sharp version of
\eqref{eq:fidelity}: the correct extremal family at fixed
$\det\rho_B$ is unknown (our numerics suggest skewed-marginal states
with aligned filters). (ii) The multipartite and
continuous-variable versions. (iii) Whether the coherence side $\chi$ of the Pythagorean
decomposition (Corollary~\ref{cor:pythagoras}) admits its own
trade-off against $p$. (iv) The connection, visible throughout, to
the determinant bookkeeping of the divisibility analysis in
\cite{indivisible} (there the determinant of the reduced propagator
crossing zero marks the \emph{indivisibility} onset; here $\det M$
prices the signal)---whether one
statement generates both is open.
